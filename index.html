I.
The Programme

What Cohort II signs up for

Venture Academy is a hands-on venture capital practicum held in association with Trinity College, Cambridge. Over eight weeks, small, cross-disciplinary teams evaluate real, live startups exactly as a fund would — building an investment thesis, stress-testing the founders, and defending their conclusions in front of the people who'd actually write the cheque.

Real-World Experience

  • Evaluate genuine early-stage startups
  • Work directly with founders, not case studies
  • Present to a live panel of investors
  • Produce a professional investment memo

Career Development

  • Build the analytical frameworks investors actually use
  • Learn from partners at active funds
  • Meet founders and investors face to face
  • Graduate as a recognised Venture Fellow

A Small, Serious Cohort

  • Twenty-five places, any college, any subject
  • Cross-disciplinary teams by design
  • Backed by Trinity College, Cambridge
  • Undergraduates, postgraduates and PhDs alike
II.
Cohort I · Lent Term 2026

Results, in numbers

23 of 25 students completed the full eight weeks. Every one of them told us what worked and what didn't — a 100% response rate on our closing survey.

Each cohort closes with a live Investment Committee. In 2026, one of four participating startups left with a £10,000 investment decision made on the spot.

Eight Weeks · Lent Term 2027

The journey

Background

How venture funds are raised, managed and invested — management fees, carried interest, and the relationship between Limited and General Partners.

Topics

Assessing founders, sizing markets, mapping competition, and reading business models — plus product defensibility, financial viability, traction and legal diligence.

The Investment

Four early-stage startups join the cohort as live subjects. Student teams work directly with founders to hone the company's proposition and pressure-test the case.

Investment Committee

In the closing week, every team presents its memo to a panel of working investors — fifteen minutes each — before the panel awards one £10,000 investment.
